# GPT Realtime Assistant — practical workflows

These are suggested user procedures, not claims that automated tests have been performed. Each workflow has a concrete outcome to inspect.

## Two people speaking different languages

Choose the two languages and enable two-way translation and spoken translations. Use one sentence or short idea per turn, especially with unfamiliar names. Wait for the response before the second speaker starts. Read the original transcript as well as the translation: a recognition mistake can propagate into a fluent but incorrect translation. Repeat reservations, addresses and numbers in short chunks.

## A meeting with action items

Select an appropriate room template, such as Daily Standup or Client Discovery. State the desired output: decisions, open questions and actions with owners. During the discussion, separate a proposal from an agreed decision. At the end, read back each action with its owner and date before saving it. Correct the notes against what participants actually confirmed.

## Explain a screenshot during a voice session

Prepare a screenshot that contains only the relevant diagram, table or error. Attach it during the session and ask one specific question, for example which column supports a stated total. Do not include passwords, access tokens or private chat history. Ask the assistant to identify visible evidence and uncertainty rather than inventing context outside the image.

## A private local Mac transcription session

Before the session, download the supported models and verify the local mode on the Apple Silicon Mac. Test with network access unavailable and non-sensitive speech. Confirm that transcription and written answers still work; do not assume that a selected model name alone proves offline readiness. Keep a cloud workflow separate rather than silently switching when local processing is slow.

## Language rehearsal with readback

Choose a short scenario, such as checking into a hotel. Ask for one question at a time and answer aloud. Compare the transcript with what you intended to say, then use readback to review the phrase. Keep a list of two or three corrections instead of treating an uninterrupted conversation as proof of fluency.
